Study Program Analysis

Every concept is built up in three layers: first in plain words, then how it actually works, then formally, with all the definitions, theorems and proofs. Along the way there are labs to play with and questions that explain every answer.

Week 1Ready

Introduction: Decidability

What a program and a program analysis are, why the halting problem and Rice's theorem make analysis impossible in general, soundness and completeness, may and must, and the JPAMB game with its wagers.
Week 2Ready

Syntactic analysis

Analysing structure instead of meaning: the levels from bits to bytecode, formal languages and grammars, the Chomsky hierarchy, regular expressions, parsers, tree-sitter queries, folds and traversals.
Week 3Ready

Semantics

Giving programs meaning: natural deduction, axiomatic, denotational and operational semantics, transition systems and traces, and the small-step semantics of the JVM that your interpreter implements.
Week 4Ready

Dynamic analysis

Learning by running: trace selection, analysis and prediction, path classes and coverage, testing and assertions, and choosing inputs with random, dictionary, small-scope and coverage-guided fuzzing.
Week 5Ready

Bounded static analysis

Running all inputs at once: the many-step function, posets and lattices, Galois connections, the sign abstraction, abstract operations, and a bounded abstract interpreter for the JVM.
Week 6Ready

Unbounded static analysis

Analysing every depth at once: dependent values and relational domains, least fixed points, the interval abstraction and its infinite chains, widening, and the worklist algorithm with reverse post-order and strongly connected components.
